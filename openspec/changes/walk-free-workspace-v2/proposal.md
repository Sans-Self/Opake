## Why

A workspace is not a folder of shared files. It is a standing *agreement* about who is a member under which key — and agreement is the oldest hard problem in distributed systems. Opake refused both of the usual ways to buy it (a trusted sequencer, which contradicts end-to-end encryption; proof-of-work, which is absurd for a dozen activists on phones), so it has to extract group agreement from a substrate built for personal data: everyone writes only to their own server, no global order exists, nothing arbitrates.

The shipped design tracks that agreement as a chain of keyring supersedes with the indexer enforcing authority at ingest. A from-scratch redesign and successive adversarial reviews stress-tested the alternatives to destruction. The result was consistent: the cryptography was never the problem, and neither was byte volume. What broke, every time, was *state-tracking logistics under two constraints acting together* — no trusted time, and every host may lie or die. Those two turn three innocuous operations into the whole difficulty: proving a served head is the current one, resolving concurrent membership changes so different observers agree, and keeping the records that make bytes findable and decryptable alive after their author's host dies.

This change writes the synthesis of that exercise into canon. It is deliberately *smaller* than the machinery it replaces, because its two load-bearing moves are subtractions — it deletes requirements rather than adding mechanisms to satisfy them.

## What Changes

- **A workspace becomes a first-class definitional concept.** A new `workspace` spec states what a workspace *is*, names the four-tier trust surface (fully trusted, socially trusted, semi-trusted, untrusted — plus *time*, trusted nowhere), and enumerates the accepted limitations that no design of ours removes. This is the conceptual anchor the other specs reference instead of re-deriving.

- **Records sign themselves.** **BREAKING** — every workspace record carries an Ed25519 signature from its author's member key. Today authorship is "whose PDS the bytes sit on"; a hostile host can forge its own user. A signature travels with the record into an archive, onto a mirror, past its author's death — which is what makes records mirrorable, closes the host-forgery flank, makes equivocation self-incriminating, and gives the blind enforcer a sound check instead of a presumption. Prerequisite: byte-recomputed CIDs ([#64](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/64)).

- **The roster is the key registry.** A workspace's roster carries each member's signing key; adding a member *is* attesting their key. Signature verification then needs no external DID-document lookup — the key is already in the snapshot being read. This matters because `did:web` (no audit log, no rotation history) is over-represented among exactly our sovereignty-minded users, so leaning on PLC for key provenance would fail them. The roster is the sole source; external DID documents aren't consulted.

- **Discard-and-retry replaces merge, scoped to membership.** A membership write names the exact record it supersedes (`supersedesCid` used as a compare-and-swap guard — it always was one, and was never used as a concurrency guard). If the parent was already superseded when the write is evaluated, the write is refused, the author's client is told, and it may replay its intent against the current state. This turns fork resolution from a data-preservation problem (which forced the CRDTs, union folds, and permanent floors this design discards) into a liveness problem. Document-tree writes keep their existing additive-merge story. Rejected writes are **never** silently discarded — this promotes "in-flight writes die silently" ([#11](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/11)) from a web-app annoyance to a protocol obligation.

- **A write is confirmed only by an independent observer.** Your own PDS returning success proves nothing — it is the untrusted party. A membership write stays *pending* until it is echoed back from somewhere that is not the author's own host: the indexer, a second relay, another member, or the author watching the firehose for its own record. The echo can carry the verdict (the set of records seen superseding the same parent), not just a receipt — so the client reaches its own conclusion and never trusts the reporter.

- **A fork-timing ceiling, measured against the frontier, not the clock.** "Late" is how far behind the live frontier a write's parent has fallen, not how long its author was offline — so a member gone a month in a quiet workspace loses nothing, while a write rooted more than a bounded distance behind a moving head is discarded. This closes the malicious "fork against a year-old ancestor" hole. It is enforceable only against a trusted freshness beacon, because timestamps are self-serving claims — which is why it depends on the sequencing capability below. (Revises an earlier "no duration threshold" framing; see design.md.)

- **Auditable sequencing, layered on top, never necessary for truth.** A new `workspace-sequencing` spec adds an append-only Merkle log of ingested record CIDs per workspace, published as signed tree heads with inclusion and consistency proofs — the `did:plc` / certificate-transparency blueprint, which the whole ecosystem already trusts as a centralized-but-auditable sequencer. It buys an auditable total order for tie-breaks, a freshness beacon (the thing that makes the fork-timing ceiling enforceable), and omission upgraded from undetectable to visible. Optional hardening: witness cosigning (C2SP `tlog-witness`) against equivocation. A self-hoster with no log falls back to the structural defence (a live frontier already refuses a fork rooted behind it); the managed offering can run the log as a service. The log can never make an invalid record valid.

- **Removal durability is built-upon, not merely witnessed, and the indexer stays an auditor.** A removal is durable once a live descendant supersedes it (or while the remover's host stays honest) — observing it is not the same as its being irreversible. Because the signed removal record sits on the remover's own untrusted PDS, a hostile host can delete it and roll it back, a *detected* liveness attack the remover re-issues against and ultimately escapes by account migration; forward-secure writes wait for durability so a revert exposes no new content. Where present, an indexer retains and re-serves the signed removal record so the head does not roll back past it — an availability help, never a truth dependency (cryptographic durability without an indexer is deferred replication, [#19](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/19)). The indexer/sequencer otherwise reports the record set and its proofs; every assertion stays client-recomputable, and none is load-bearing for writing or truth.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `workspace`: What a workspace is (a standing membership-under-a-key agreement), the four-tier trust surface, and the accepted limitations no construction removes (freshness is a liveness property never a proof; silence is invisible; agreement is only eventual; small groups get no arithmetic).
- `record-signatures`: Member-key Ed25519 signatures on every workspace record; the signed cleartext governance envelope that lets a keyless enforcer reject forgeries soundly; equivocation as self-incriminating evidence; canonical-byte signing over the same bytes the CID pins ([#64](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/64)).
- `workspace-sequencing`: The auditable transparency log — signed tree heads, inclusion and consistency proofs, the freshness beacon, witness cosigning — layered, optional, and never a trust root.

### Modified Capabilities
- `workspace-membership`: Discard-and-retry via `supersedesCid` compare-and-swap (membership-scoped); write-confirmation by an independent observer with never-silent-discard; the fork-timing ceiling; frontier-scoped endorsement head selection with an ungrindable VRF tie-break resolved at the most-recent common ancestor (not lowest-CID, which is mineable); the roster carrying each member's signing and VRF keys.
- `workspace-identity`: Roster-as-key-registry as the sole key-provenance source ([#57](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/57)); external DID documents (PLC / `did:web`) not consulted for signing-key provenance at all; founder-residue in the identity tag recorded as a parked limitation for the eventual rotation work ([#18](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/18)).
- `indexer-consistency`: The indexer/sequencer reports the record set and its proofs as an auditor; the echo carries the compare-and-swap verdict; omission is made visible by consistency proofs; nothing it asserts is load-bearing for writing or truth.
- `record-validity`: Signature verification as a validity gate, and how it composes with the read-lenient / write-strict posture already in place — including that keyring-adoption surfaces keep `workspace-identity`'s silent-drop precedence.
- `keyring-tombstones`: Rollback restored-head selection moves from the indexer's `indexed_at` to the client-recomputable endorsement rule, and rollback is bounded by the witnessed-removal durability property so a delete cannot silently reverse a witnessed removal.
- `lineage`: The `supersedesCid` content pin is verified by byte-recomputation ([#64](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/64)), closing the v1 tampered-bytes-with-true-CID gap that this change's signing requires closed anyway.
- `workspace-key-rotation`: The self-sufficient rotation write set carries every remaining member's signing key and is author-signed, so a rotation head can still authenticate the roster.
- `auth-identity`: The Ed25519 verifying key and a mnemonic-derived VRF key become required in `publicKey/self` — the keys a manager reads to attest a member into the roster.
- `tree-chains`: Directory fork resolution breaks ties by the ungrindable VRF tie-break instead of author-controlled `createdAt` or mineable CID (an earlier draft's lowest-CID claim was wrong — CIDs are grindable); directory records are confirmed in signature scope (formalizing an assumption the spec already made).

## Impact

- **Federation-class.** Per project rule, the spec delta is red-penned before any implementation begins. No code lands from this proposal until the delta is reviewed.
- **Wire format.** New signature field(s) on `at.opake.keyring` (and the signed governance envelope); a new sequencing/tree-head record or indexer-published artifact. Lexicon JSON, `OPAKE_COLLECTIONS`, and the auth permission set all move together (CLAUDE.md #13).
- **Crypto.** Ed25519 record signing over canonical dag-cbor; depends on byte-recomputed CID verification ([#64](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/64)) shipping first. A mnemonic-derived VRF key (ECVRF over Ed25519, RFC 9381; its own HKDF path) for the ungrindable fork tie-break, published in `publicKey/self` and attested into the roster. No change to the proven encryption layer (group-key wrap, content-key hierarchy, rotation-aware reads).
- **Indexer (Elixir).** `authority.ex` gains signature verification at ingest; a new transparency-log consumer/publisher (Merkle log, signed tree heads, proof endpoints); the echo/verdict on the write path.
- **Client.** Pending-write state and honest UI for it (WASM/SDK/react); replay-on-reject; frontier cache promoted from optimization to load-bearing member state ([#69](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/69)). CLI daemon confirms writes without keepers.
- **Dissolves / reframes tracker items.** [#19](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/19) custody (the hard half — record custody — largely dissolves once signed records are mirrorable); [#68](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/68) chain-archives (purpose partly evaporates if onboarding never touches history — reassess to audit-availability only); [#11](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/11) (promoted to protocol obligation); [#57](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/57) (answered by roster-as-registry); [#64](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/64) (hard prerequisite); [#18](https://github.com/Opake-at/Opake/issues/18) rotation (stakes raised, stays out of scope here but flagged).
- **Non-goals unchanged.** Membership privacy, historical-access revocation, malicious-member containment, real-time collaborative editing, and protocol-level quota/billing all remain out of scope.
