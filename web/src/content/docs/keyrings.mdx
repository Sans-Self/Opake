## The "Group Key" Concept

In a traditional encrypted app, if you want to share a folder with 10 people, you have to encrypt every file for 10 different public keys. If you add an 11th person, you have to re-encrypt everything. This is slow, inefficient, and doesn't scale.

Opake solves this with **Keyrings**.

A Keyring is a named group that has its own **Group Key (GK)**. Instead of encrypting files for individuals, you encrypt them for the Keyring.

1. The file's `Content Key` is wrapped under the **Group Key**.
2. The **Group Key** itself is then wrapped for each individual member of the group.

<Callout type="info">
  **The Result:** When a new member joins the group, we only need to wrap the Group Key for them
  *once*. They immediately gain access to every file ever encrypted for that Keyring.
</Callout>

## 1. Creating a Keyring

A Keyring is its own record on the AT Protocol (`app.opake.keyring`).

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">

    1. Click the **(+)** icon in the Sidebar and select **New Keyring**.
    2. Give it a name (e.g., "Family Photos").
    3. Opake generates a new Group Key, wraps it to your public key, and publishes the record.

  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake keyring create "Family Photos"</CodeBlock>
  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

## 2. Managing Membership

Adding and removing members is a cryptographic operation.

### Adding a Member

When you add someone to a Keyring, you are simply taking the Group Key and wrapping it to their public key.

- **Effect:** They can now open the "Group Lockbox" and decrypt any file associated with that Keyring.

### Removing a Member (Key Rotation)

This is the most complex part of the system. To ensure the removed member can't access _future_ files, Opake performs a **Key Rotation**:

1. It generates a **NEW** Group Key (`GK_n+1`).
2. it wraps this new key for all _remaining_ members.
3. It keeps a history of the old keys so that older files can still be decrypted by the remaining group.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Forward Secrecy Reality:** Just like with [Grants](/docs/sharing-dids), removing someone from a
  Keyring prevents them from accessing *future* files. If they already downloaded and decrypted old
  files, those files remain in their possession.
</Callout>

---

## 3. Uploading to a Keyring

When you upload a file, you can choose to associate it with a Keyring instead of an individual person.

<CodeBlock language="sh">opake upload "budget.pdf" --keyring "Family Photos"</CodeBlock>

Every member of that Keyring will see the file in their [Inbox](/docs/sharing-dids) and can decrypt it instantly.
