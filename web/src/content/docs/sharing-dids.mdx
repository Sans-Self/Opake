<Lead>
  In the traditional cloud, sharing a file means granting a server permission to show your data to
  someone else. In Opake, sharing means giving a key to your file to another user.
</Lead>

## The "Grant" Model

When you share a file with someone in Opake, you are creating a **Grant**.

Think of a Grant as a small, specialized lockbox. Inside this box is the `Content Key` for your file. This lockbox is specially designed so that only the recipient's private key can open it. Once you've created this box, you leave it on the network. The recipient can then pick it up, open it with their key, and use the `Content Key` to decrypt the file directly from your PDS.

<Callout type="info">
  **Zero Server Involvement:** Your PDS (and the recipient's PDS) act only as couriers. They never
  see the key, and they never see the file content.
</Callout>

---

## 1. Sharing with a Handle

To share a file, you just need the recipient's AT Protocol handle (e.g., `@bob.bsky.social`).

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">

    1. Select a file in your cabinet.
    2. Click the **Share** icon.
    3. Enter the recipient's handle.
    4. Opake resolves their handle to a DID, fetches their public encryption key, wraps the file key, and publishes the Grant record.

  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake share photo.jpg bob.bsky.social</CodeBlock>
  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

## 2. Why DIDs Matter

You might know your friend as `@bob.bsky.social`, but Opake knows them as `did:plc:z724xy...`.

A handle is just a nickname that can change. A **DID (Decentralized Identifier)** is a permanent, cryptographic ID. By sharing to a DID, the access remains valid even if your friend moves to a different PDS or changes their handle.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Public Keys are Public:** Opake automatically publishes your public encryption key to your PDS
  when you log in. This is how others can wrap files to you without needing to ask for your
  "address" first.
</Callout>

---

## 3. Revoking Access

If you want to stop sharing a file, you simply delete the Grant record.

However, because Opake is truly decentralized, there is a nuance to revocation. Once a recipient has decrypted a file, they have a copy of the plaintext. Deleting a Grant prevents them from fetching _future_ updates or re-downloading the file if they lose their local copy, but it cannot "reach out" and delete the data from their physical device.
