<ChapterHeader title="What your key actually looks like" />

<Lead>
  When Opake sets up your identity, it shows you twenty-four ordinary English words. Those
  words *are* your key — write them down and you can bring your identity back on any device,
  at any time. Lose them and nothing in the world can help you.
</Lead>

## What Is It?

When you first set up Opake, the app picks 24 words at random from a fixed list of around
2000 common English words. Those 24 words carry enough randomness that no other person on
earth will ever land on the same sequence. Your identity is unique by construction.

Type the same 24 words back in on a different device (a new phone, a fresh laptop, a rebuild
after you reinstalled the OS) and Opake rebuilds the exact same keys from them. Same words
in, same identity out.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Write it down. Store it safely. Never share it.** Your 24 words are the only way back
  into your cabinet if you lose access to every device you're signed in on. Opake can't
  recover them for you; there's nobody behind the scenes holding a backup.
</Callout>

---

## How It Works

Same words in, same keys out; every time, on every device.

Opake runs your 24 words through a fixed, slow-by-design process. "Fixed" means you get
identical keys every time you type the phrase, which is how old files still decrypt on a new
phone. "Slow-by-design" means it takes a computer more effort to turn the words into a key — fine
for you (a second or two of wait on first setup) but very difficult for someone trying to
guess your words.

<Callout type="info">
  For the specific algorithms behind this derivation, see [Encryption & Keys](/docs/encryption).
</Callout>

## When You First Set Up

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">
    After signing in, Opake will display your 24 words in a numbered grid. You can:

    - **Copy** them to your clipboard
    - **Download** them as a `.txt` file (saved so only your user account can read it)

    You'll then be asked to confirm 3 randomly chosen words to prove you've saved them.

  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    On your first login, the CLI displays your 24 words in a numbered grid:

    ```
     1. mimic          7. action         13. zebra          19. crawl
     2. short          8. bundle         14. rain           20. pen
     3. have           9. wagon          15. addict         21. flavor
     4. picnic        10. wrong          16. legend         22. useful
     5. neck          11. balance        17. swallow        23. caution
     6. awful         12. palm           18. space          24. focus
    ```

    You can save this to a file when prompted. You'll then confirm 3 words to verify you've
    recorded them.

  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

After confirmation, Opake derives your keys and publishes the public half to your PDS. The
words themselves are never stored and never shown again.

## Recovering on a New Device

If you get a new device (or reinstall), you can bring your identity back without needing the
old device at all.

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">
    After signing in, choose **"Use your recovery phrase"** and enter your 24 words in the grid.
    You can also paste all 24 words at once — the app distributes them across the fields
    automatically.
  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake recover</CodeBlock>

    Or import from a saved `.txt` file:

    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake recover --file ~/opake-seed-phrase.txt</CodeBlock>

  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

If the key Opake rebuilds doesn't match the public half that's already published to your
PDS, Opake will warn you. That usually means one of two things: a typo in the phrase, or an
older account that was set up before 24-word phrases were the default.

## 24 Words vs. Device Pairing

Both get your identity onto a new device. The difference:

|                          | 24 Words                            | Device Pairing                         |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Requires old device?** | No                                  | Yes                                    |
| **Works offline?**       | Partially (need PDS for publishing) | No (PDS relay required)                |
| **Input method**         | Type or paste 24 words              | Approve on existing device             |
| **Best for**             | Disaster recovery, first-time setup | Quick setup when you have both devices |

Device pairing is faster when you have both devices handy. The 24 words are your safety net
when you don't.

## Storing Your Words

Some options, in rough order of paranoia:

- **Paper in a safe** — low-tech, survives digital disasters
- **Password manager** — convenient, but introduces a dependency
- **Metal backup plate** — survives fire and flood, if you're that kind of person

What you should _not_ do:

- Store it in a cloud notes app (defeats the purpose)
- Take a screenshot (phone backups aren't end-to-end encrypted by default)
- Email it to yourself

<Callout type="info">
  **The `.txt` file** saved during CLI setup is set up so only your user account can read it
  — a reasonable short-term backup. Still, move the words somewhere more permanent and delete
  the file when you can.
</Callout>
