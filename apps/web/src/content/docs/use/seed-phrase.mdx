<Lead>
  Your seed phrase is the master key to your entire Opake identity. Twenty-four words that can
  reconstruct your encryption keys on any device, at any time.
</Lead>

## What Is It?

When you first set up Opake, the app generates 24 random words from a standardised wordlist (BIP-39). These words encode 256 bits of entropy — the mathematical seed from which your encryption and signing keys are derived.

The same 24 words will always produce the same keys. This means you can recover your entire identity on a new device by entering the same phrase, without needing access to your old device.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Write it down. Store it safely. Never share it.** Your seed phrase is the only way to recover
  your identity if you lose access to all your devices. Opake cannot recover it for you.
</Callout>

---

## How It Works

When you enter your seed phrase, Opake runs it through a deterministic derivation pipeline:

1. **PBKDF2** stretches the phrase into a 512-bit master seed (this is slow on purpose — it makes brute-force attacks expensive).
2. **HKDF** derives two separate keys from that seed:
   - An **X25519 key** for encrypting and decrypting files
   - An **Ed25519 key** for signing and authenticating

The important part: this process is entirely deterministic. Same words in, same keys out. Every time, on every device.

## When You First Set Up

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">
    After signing in, Opake will display your 24 words in a numbered grid. You can:

    - **Copy** them to your clipboard
    - **Download** them as a `.txt` file (saved with restricted permissions)

    You'll then be asked to confirm 3 randomly chosen words to prove you've saved them.

  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    On your first login, the CLI displays your seed phrase in a numbered grid:

    ```
     1. mimic          7. action         13. zebra          19. crawl
     2. short          8. bundle         14. rain           20. pen
     3. have           9. wagon          15. addict         21. flavor
     4. picnic        10. wrong          16. legend         22. useful
     5. neck          11. balance        17. swallow        23. caution
     6. awful         12. palm           18. space          24. focus
    ```

    You can save this to a file when prompted. You'll then confirm 3 words to verify you've recorded them.

  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

After confirmation, Opake derives your keys and publishes the public key to your PDS. The seed phrase is never stored or shown again.

## Recovering on a New Device

If you get a new device (or reinstall), you can recover your identity without needing your old device.

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">
    After signing in, choose **"Use your recovery phrase"** and enter your 24 words in the grid.
    You can also paste all 24 words at once — the app will distribute them across the fields automatically.
  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake recover</CodeBlock>

    Or import from a saved `.txt` file:

    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake recover --file ~/opake-seed-phrase.txt</CodeBlock>

  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

If the derived key doesn't match the one published on your PDS, Opake will warn you. This usually means either the phrase is wrong, or the account was set up before seed phrases were the default.

## Seed Phrase vs. Device Pairing

Both accomplish the same goal — getting your keys onto a new device. The difference:

|                          | Seed Phrase                         | Device Pairing                         |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Requires old device?** | No                                  | Yes                                    |
| **Works offline?**       | Partially (need PDS for publishing) | No (PDS relay required)                |
| **Input method**         | Type or paste 24 words              | Approve on existing device             |
| **Best for**             | Disaster recovery, first-time setup | Quick setup when you have both devices |

Device pairing is faster when you have both devices handy. The seed phrase is your safety net when you don't.

## Storing Your Seed Phrase

Some options, in rough order of paranoia:

- **Paper in a safe** — low-tech, survives digital disasters
- **Password manager** — convenient, but introduces a dependency
- **Metal backup plate** — survives fire and flood, if you're that kind of person

What you should _not_ do:

- Store it in a cloud notes app (defeats the purpose)
- Take a screenshot (phone backups are not encrypted end-to-end by default)
- Email it to yourself

<Callout type="info">
  **The `.txt` file** saved during setup uses restrictive file permissions (0600 on Unix). It's a
  reasonable short-term backup, but you should transfer the words to a more permanent medium and
  delete the file.
</Callout>
