<Lead>
  Sharing on a traditional cloud means granting a server permission to show your data to someone.
  Sharing in Opake means handing someone a key to your file. Different model, different threat
  profile.
</Lead>

## The Grant model

When you share a file, you create a **Grant** — a small record containing the file's content
key, encrypted so that only the recipient's private key can open it. You publish the grant
record to your own PDS. The recipient finds it via the indexer, unwraps the key with their
private key, and then fetches the encrypted file directly from your PDS.

Neither PDS sees the key or the file content. They're just relaying ciphertext between two
clients.

---

## 1. Sharing with a Handle

To share a file, you just need the recipient's AT Protocol handle (e.g., `@bob.bsky.social`).

<PlatformToggle>
  <PlatformTab name="Web App">

    1. Select a file in your cabinet.
    2. Click the **Share** icon.
    3. Enter the recipient's handle.
    4. Opake resolves their handle to a DID, fetches their public encryption key, wraps the file key, and publishes the Grant record.

  </PlatformTab>
  <PlatformTab name="CLI">
    <CodeBlock language="sh">opake share photo.jpg bob.bsky.social</CodeBlock>
  </PlatformTab>
</PlatformToggle>

## 2. Why DIDs matter

You might know your friend as `@bob.bsky.social`, but Opake stores them internally as
`did:plc:z724xy...`.

A handle is a nickname that can change. A **DID (Decentralized Identifier)** is a permanent,
cryptographic ID. Grants are keyed to DIDs, so access stays valid if your friend moves to a
different PDS or swaps their handle.

<Callout type="info">
  Opake publishes your public encryption key to your PDS when you first log in. That's how
  someone else's client can wrap a file to you without you having to exchange an address
  out-of-band.
</Callout>

---

## 3. Revoking access

To stop sharing a file, delete the grant record.

There's an important nuance. Once a recipient has decrypted a file, they have the plaintext
locally. Deleting the grant prevents them from fetching future updates or re-downloading if
they lose their copy, but it can't reach out across the network to delete what they already
have on disk.
